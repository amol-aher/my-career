Resolving Senior-Level Technical Disagreements

Principles

Disagreements between senior engineers are healthy when handled constructively. My approach prioritizes facts, outcomes, and maintaining working relationships. Key principles:

  • Listen first — understand the other person's goals and constraints.
  • Move from opinions to data — quantify costs, performance, operational overhead, and time-to-delivery.
  • Prototype to de-risk — a small spike often reveals the better option.
  • Favor reversible decisions — use feature flags, abstractions, or adapters so choices can be changed later.
  • Escalate only when necessary — involve an unbiased architect or decision-maker if consensus can't be reached.

Practical process

  1. Run 1:1s to capture perspectives and underlying assumptions.
  2. Define decision criteria (cost, performance, developer velocity, security, maintenance).
  3. Collect data (benchmarks, TCO estimate, implementation effort).
  4. Prototype minimal spike if uncertainty remains.
  5. Make a timeboxed decision and document rationale in an ADR (Architecture Decision Record).
  6. Implement with safeguards (feature flag, canary rollout, monitoring).

Subquestions & Answers

What if the other engineer refuses to compromise?

Keep the conversation respectful. Escalate to a neutral senior or architect, present data, and propose a timeboxed trial so neither side "loses".

How do you ensure the decision is respected afterward?

Document the ADR, communicate to stakeholders, assign ownership for follow-ups, and set metrics to validate the decision.

Conclusion

Disagreements are an opportunity to improve decisions. Use data, prototypes, and a structured decision process to arrive at the best, reversible outcome while preserving team trust.
