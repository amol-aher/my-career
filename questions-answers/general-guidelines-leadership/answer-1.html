Leadership Style

My style is collaborative and pragmatic. I aim to enable engineers to do their best work by removing blockers, setting clear priorities, and creating feedback loops. I pair systems thinking with people-first leadership — focusing on psychological safety, clarity of goals, and measurable outcomes.

Concrete Examples

  • Mentorship: Ran a quarterly mentorship program: pairing senior engineers with mid-level ones, measuring progress with technical checkpoints and shipping ownership metrics.
  • Process improvement: Introduced trunk-based feature-flag driven development and reduced release-time regressions by 45% in six months.
  • On-call and incidents: Reworked the on-call rotations, added runbooks, and cut MTTD by 70% and MTTR by 50% through structured drills and postmortems.

Subquestions & Answers

How do you measure team health and success?

Combination of qualitative and quantitative metrics: velocity trends (but not as a proxy for productivity), deployment frequency, lead time for changes, mean time to restore (MTTR), defect rates, sprint predictability, engineering NPS, and anonymized engagement surveys.

How do you handle underperforming team members?

Use structured feedback: clear examples, set SMART improvement goals, provide mentorship or training, and follow a 30/60/90 day review cadence with documented outcomes. If insufficient improvement, work with HR for the next steps.

How do you resolve conflicts within the team?

Start with private 1:1s to understand perspectives, bring parties together for facilitated discussion, focus on facts and desired outcomes, and create clear action items and follow-ups.
