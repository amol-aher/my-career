Balance Between Coding and Managing

Overview

The split between coding and managing varies by role level and company stage. Typical patterns:

  • Early-stage / small team lead: ~60–80% coding, 20–40% management/coordination.
  • Mid-stage (tech lead / engineering manager): ~30–60% coding, 40–70% management.
  • Senior manager / Director: ~10–30% coding, 70–90% management/strategy.

My approach

I follow a context-driven approach: if the team requires technical direction for a new architecture, I increase hands-on coding and architectural prototypes. During stable delivery phases I scale back to unblock, mentor, and manage stakeholders.

How I allocate my time (example week)

Mon: 40% meetings (planning, stakeholders), 30% code review, 30% coding
Tue: 20% deep coding, 30% 1:1s, 20% design review, 30% backlog grooming
Wed: 40% delivering roadmap tasks, 30% collaborating on architecture, 30% mentoring
Thu: 40% meetings (HR, hiring), 30% code & PR reviews, 30% docs & runbooks
Fri: 50% heads-down coding & unblock tasks, 25% team demo and retrospectives, 25% learning and hiring

Subquestions & Answers

How do you keep technical credibility?

By doing periodic deep-dive coding sessions, leading architecture spikes, and staying current with language and infra updates. I aim to ship meaningful code and review high-impact PRs to remain technically fluent.

How do you prevent becoming a bottleneck?

Delegate via clear ownership, documentation, and training. Use design reviews rather than always making final decisions; cultivate senior engineers as tech leads.
