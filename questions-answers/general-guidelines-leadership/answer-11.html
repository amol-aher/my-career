Low‑key Areas — What Didn’t Work Out Well at Jombay

Summary

Every role has tradeoffs. At Jombay there were areas that taught me important lessons: technical debt accumulation during fast growth, occasional misalignment between product and engineering priorities, and limited formal career ladders for mid-level engineers during a scaling phase.

What specifically didn’t work well

  • Rising technical debt: Rapid feature delivery prioritized short-term shipping; several core services accrued debt in the form of brittle integrations and insufficient test coverage.
  • Product/engineering misalignment: On a few initiatives, product pushed scope without rescoping technical impact which led to delayed launches and frustrated engineers.
  • Onboarding variability: New hires sometimes lacked consistent onboarding paths, causing an uneven ramp-up time.
  • Limited documentation culture: Knowledge lived in a few engineers' heads rather than documented runbooks or architecture docs.

How I addressed / helped mitigate these issues

  • Introduced a small "debt reduction" quarterly sprint to pay down high‑risk technical debt and mandated a bit of refactoring in adjacent feature work.
  • Set up structured product-engineering syncs and architecture reviews to assess technical impacts before commitments.
  • Built a basic onboarding checklist and a peer-mentor program which cut initial ramp time by anecdotally ~30%.
  • Started an internal docs effort (Confluence/Markdown) and paired it with a PR gating rule that major infra changes require an architecture note.

What I learned

Shipping fast is important, but sustainable velocity requires discipline: architecture hygiene, shared understanding, and time-boxed debt repayment. Clear expectations and explicit documentation are force multipliers.
