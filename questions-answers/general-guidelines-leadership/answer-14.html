Disagreeing with a Technical Decision — Example & Approach

Situation

On one project at Jombay, product wanted to adopt a third‑party search product quickly. The team favored a hosted solution to speed delivery. I was concerned about long‑term cost, vendor lock‑in, and limitations for custom ranking logic.

Actions I took

  1. Gathered data: Estimated TCO for 3 years, performance expectations, and integration effort.
  2. Built a small spike: Created a quick prototype using an open-source search (Elasticsearch) to validate feasibility and integration complexity.
  3. Presented tradeoffs: Laid out TCO, operational overhead, and roadmap limitations for both hosted and self-managed approaches.
  4. Suggested compromise: Proceed with hosted search for initial MVP but design abstractions (adapter layer) so swapping later would be straightforward. Added monitoring and cost triggers to revisit the decision.

Outcome

Team accepted the compromise. We shipped faster with the hosted option while keeping technical flexibility. Six months later, based on usage and cost, we re-evaluated and migrated core, high‑volume indexes to a managed cluster plus caching for hot queries.

General approach to disagreements

  • Be data-driven: quantify performance, cost, and risk.
  • Prototype where useful to de-risk decisions.
  • Focus on options and tradeoffs, not personalities.
  • Find a compromise that keeps future options open (abstraction, feature flags, adapters).
