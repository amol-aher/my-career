Defining Microservice Boundaries

Deciding where to draw microservice boundaries is both a technical and organizational decision. The goal is to maximize cohesion within a service and minimize coupling between services.

Primary principles

  • Domain-Driven Design (DDD) — Bounded Contexts: Services should align to business capabilities (billing, user-auth, catalog).
  • Single Responsibility / Cohesion: A service should own a single business capability end-to-end — API, data, and logic.
  • Data ownership: Each service should have its own data store to avoid cross-service coupling at DB level.
  • Team per service: Organize teams around services (Conway's law) so a service maps to a small, autonomous team.
  • Independent deployability: Services must be deployable independently without coordinated releases.

Practical heuristics (questions to ask)

  1. Does this capability change at different rates than others?
  2. Can we test and deploy it independently?
  3. Does it have its own data lifecycle and privacy/regulatory needs?
  4. Is it composed of synchronous transactions across many other capabilities?

Example boundaries

For an e-commerce system, reasonable services: catalog service, pricing, cart, checkout/payment, user/auth, fulfillment. Each owns its DB and emits events for other services to react.

Subquestions you may be asked

How big should a service be? Small enough to be understood by a small team (2–8 engineers), large enough to avoid chatty network calls.

When to split or merge? Split when a service becomes hard to understand or scale differently. Merge when operations/complexity overhead outweighs benefits.

Checklist for boundary decision

  • Align with business capability
  • Independent data and lifecycle
  • Minimal synchronous cross-service calls
  • Automated tests and CI for independent deploys
