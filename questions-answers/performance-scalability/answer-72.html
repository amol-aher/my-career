Active-Active vs Active-Passive Architectures

Active-Active

All regions are serving traffic simultaneously. Requires data replication (multi-master or eventual consistency) and conflict resolution. Use DynamoDB Global Tables, CockroachDB, or design for commutative operations.

Active-Passive

Primary serves all traffic; secondary remains on standby and is promoted on failover. Easier to reason about data consistency, often implemented with cross-region read replicas and promotion scripts.

Implementation steps (example)

Active-active with DynamoDB Global Tables

1. Create DynamoDB tables as global tables across regions.
2. Deploy app instances in both regions and use latency-based routing.
3. Ensure operations are idempotent and resolve conflicts if necessary.

Active-passive with Aurora

1. Aurora cluster in primary region with Global DB replication to secondary region.
2. Route traffic to primary via Route53. Monitor health.
3. On failover, promote secondary and update DNS to point to new writer.

Considerations

  • Active-active reduces RTO but increases complexity (conflict handling).
  • Active-passive is simpler but may have higher RTO and longer failover processes.
