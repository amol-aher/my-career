Handling Obscure or Lacking Requirements

Unclear requirements are common. The goal is to reduce ambiguity fast, minimize wasted work, and align stakeholders. Below is a practical approach and likely subquestions.

1. Ask targeted clarifying questions

Use examples, acceptance criteria, and "given/when/then" scenarios. Example:

Given a returning user with a cart,
When they apply coupon X,
Then discount Y should be applied to the line-items only, not shipping.

2. Propose assumptions and get buy-in

Document assumptions as testable items and get stakeholder sign-off (Jira ticket comments or a short PR-like spec).

3. Deliver a spike or prototype

Build a small prototype or spike to validate assumptions and reveal hidden complexities.

4. Use iterative delivery

Break work into small deliverables with clear acceptance criteria so feedback can course-correct quickly.

5. Fall-back: Decision log

Record decisions and rationale in a decision log (ADR — Architecture Decision Record) so future changes know why choices were made.

Common subquestions & answers

  • Q: What if stakeholders disagree? A: Show prototypes and data; prioritize based on business impact.
  • Q: What if time is limited? A: Deliver the simplest thing that could possibly work and iterate.
