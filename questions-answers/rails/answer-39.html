Challenges in Moving from Monolith to Microservices

1. Complexity Management

  • Increased Operational Overhead: Managing multiple services requires sophisticated orchestration
  • Debugging Difficulty: Distributed tracing becomes essential for diagnosing issues across services
  • Coordination Challenges: Ensuring consistent deployment and rollback strategies

2. Data Management Challenges

Challenge Impact Mitigation Strategy
Database Splitting Referential integrity breaks Eventual consistency with sagas, CQRS
Shared Data Access Performance bottlenecks Cache frequently accessed data, use read replicas
Transaction Management Inconsistent states Saga pattern for distributed transactions

3. Organizational Challenges

  • Team Structure Changes: Shift from component teams to cross-functional product teams
  • Communication Overhead: Increased coordination between independent teams
  • Knowledge Silos: Risk of losing institutional knowledge during decomposition

4. Deployment and Testing

  • Integration Testing Complexity: Need for comprehensive contract testing between services
  • Deployment Coordination: Coordinating releases across dependent services
  • Rollback Difficulties: Partial rollbacks become more complex than monolithic rollbacks

5. Cost Implications

  • Infrastructure Costs: Higher operational costs for managing multiple services
  • Tooling Investment: Significant investment in observability and DevOps tooling
  • Training Requirements: Team retraining for distributed systems concepts

Common Subquestions:

Q: How do you handle service dependencies?

Implement service meshes (Istio, Linkerd) for traffic management, use circuit breakers to prevent cascading failures, and establish clear SLAs between services.

Q: What's your approach to versioning microservices?

Use semantic versioning with backward-compatible changes. Implement API gateways for version routing and deprecate old versions gradually.

Q: How do you maintain security in a distributed architecture?

Implement mutual TLS authentication, centralize security policies with service mesh, conduct regular penetration testing, and enforce least privilege principles.
