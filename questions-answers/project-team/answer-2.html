Handling feedback — giving and receiving

System architect · Technical lead · Senior software engineer (Ruby on Rails, AWS, SQL, Vue.js)

Summary: Feedback is a tool for growth. I practice structured, empathetic, and actionable feedback both ways.

Giving feedback
  1. Use SBI (Situation-Behavior-Impact): Describe the situation, the observable behavior, and the impact on team/product.
  2. Be specific & timely: Don't wait weeks; give feedback close to the event.
  3. Balance praise and improvement: Use “Start/Stop/Continue” for actionable guidance.
  4. Follow-up: Agree on measurable steps and revisit in 1:1s.
Receiving feedback
  1. Listen actively: Avoid defending; ask clarifying questions.
  2. Paraphrase: "If I understand you correctly, you felt...".
  3. Action plan: Convert feedback into 1–3 concrete actions, add them to personal goals, and update stakeholders on progress.
  4. Thank and close the loop: Acknowledge the feedback and report back when changes are made.
Sample script for a difficult feedback conversation
Manager: "When you push large PRs without split commits (situation), it's harder to review and increases risk (behavior). That led to longer review cycles and a staging regression (impact)."
You: "Thanks — I see the impact. I'll split large changes into smaller PRs and add a PR checklist. Can we revisit this in two weeks in the 1:1?"
Common follow-up subquestions (answered proactively)
  • How often? Weekly or bi-weekly 1:1s, quarterly performance reviews.
  • Public vs private? Praise publicly, corrective feedback privately.
  • Documentation? Document key feedback and actions in personal development notes.
